## Principal timing, low dots and explicit ornament reduction

The source prints **1=C, 4/4 and quarter note = 82**. The compiler's absolute
pitch convention is undotted 1 = C4, with upper/lower dots changing one octave.
It is a notation convention, not a claim about the singer's recorded register.
The selected **G3–D5** pitches fit the fixed natural-note F3–E6 instrument with
**zero semitone transposition, zero global octave shift and no local octave
folds or pitch alterations**. The selected principal vocabulary is C, D, E,
G and A; no essential accidentals are removed.

Low G3 remains at selected beats 14, 38, 62 and 86. Low A3 remains at beats
13, 16, 37, 40, 61, 64, 85, 101 and 117. The later tonic cadences at beats
102 and 118 are C4, unlike the earlier G3 endings. Neither an earlier cadence
nor a familiar repeated phrase may overwrite a later occurrence.

All **16 adjacent same-pitch pairs are true separate attacks**. In particular,
the final C4 at beat 117.5 lasts 0.5 beat and the next C4 at beat 118 lasts two
beats; they are not one 2.5-beat tie. Multi-pitch arcs, including G–A–G with
same-pitch endpoints, are slurs and retain their intermediate attacks. The
shortest principal duration and onset spacing are 0.25 quarter beat. Each
source bar independently fills exactly four beats; correct totals do not by
themselves establish correct internal rhythm.

**Five small, unquantified G4 grace notes are deliberately omitted**, each
before the full-size E4 in source m14, m20, m26, m30 and m34. The corresponding
selected E4 onsets are 35, 59, 83, 99 and 115; each retains its entire one-beat
duration. No grace duration is guessed, no principal time is stolen, and no
later onset moves. The similar principal figure in m8 has no such grace and
does not authorize a sixth omission. This is an explicitly ornament-reduced
principal-note practice edition, not an ornament-complete literal performance.

The last attack C4@118/2 ends exactly at **120**. There is no final rest, extra
repeat, appended tail, or rounding to a longer bar. A plucked sample's natural
decay is separate from notated duration and is not a source rest.

## Tempo, saved preferences and suitability

**82 BPM is this edition's printed source tempo**, replacing the historical
80 BPM. It is not a new arbitrary practice tempo. The canonical id,
`defaultSpeedFactor: 0.9` and `difficulty: medium` remain unchanged. An existing
saved speed multiplier remains the user's choice; adopting a new BPM does not
rewrite that setting. The metadata suggestion is not proof of the browser's
actual initial or remembered multiplier.

The notated 120 beats last approximately **87.80 seconds at 82 BPM / 1.0x**,
or **97.56 seconds at the retained 0.9 suggestion**, before preparation and
audio decay. The old 72 beats at 80 BPM lasted 54 seconds at 1.0x; the longer
selection deliberately keeps the entire printed vocal line rather than
optimizing for a shorter exercise.

Suitability remains **conditional**. The 0.25-beat minimum is approximately
182.93 ms at source tempo and 203.25 ms at 0.9. The largest adjacent pitch leap
is seven semitones. On the app's displayed tine layout the maximum adjacent
index distance is 12 and the shortest same-side onset spacing is also
0.25 beat. Side inference is not an observed hand choice. Sixteenth-note
cells, rapid true reattacks and register changes need slow, sectional practice
and physical testing; lower notes and matching source data do not establish
that this edition is easier.

Two independent source readers inspected the same fixed image before comparing
musical tuples. All 196 pitch/onset/duration tuples, all 30 bars, all five
grace omissions and the selected boundary agree. This is **two independent
readings of one publication**, not two independent published sources.
The implementation worker also viewed the actual full-page image.
